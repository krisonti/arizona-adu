Questions

The straight answers.

What does an addition cost?

There's no fixed price, because additions vary far more than a standalone unit — a straightforward bump-out and a full primary suite with a spa bath are very different projects. Cost depends on the size, the scope, whether we build out or build up, and the finishes you choose. That's why we give you a firm, parcel-specific estimate for free before you commit to anything, rather than a made-up starting number.

How long does it take?

Every addition runs through the same three phases — design, permitting, then construction. Design and city permitting come first, and the build follows once permits are issued. Timelines depend on the size and scope of the addition and the city's review pace; we lay out a realistic schedule for your specific project up front, so you're not guessing.

Do I need a permit for an addition?

Yes. Additions change your home's structure and footprint, so they require permits, structural engineering, and city inspections. Handling that process is the core of what SOKO does — we prepare the drawings and engineering, run the submittal, and manage inspections through to final sign-off.

Can you match my existing home?

That's the whole point. We design the addition to tie into your existing rooflines, exterior finish, and interior details so it reads as part of the original home rather than an obvious add-on. On many projects — like the one shown above — we also update the existing house so old and new match cleanly.

Can I live in the house during construction?

Usually, yes. Because most of the work happens on the new footprint, we can seal off the construction zone and keep the rest of your home livable. There are noisier, dustier stretches — especially when we connect the new space to the existing structure — and we'll walk you through what to expect and plan around it.

Should I build out or build up?

A single-story "build-out" extends your footprint across the lot and is typically simpler and less expensive — great when you have the yard for it. A two-story "build-up" adds space above the existing home when the lot is tight or you want to preserve outdoor space. We'll tell you which makes sense for your house, lot, and budget.

How is an addition financed?

Most homeowners fund an addition through their home's equity — a HELOC or cash-out refinance — or through a renovation loan; some pay cash. We're not a lender and this isn't financial advice, but we're happy to talk through the common paths on your consult so you can take real numbers to a lender.

Where do you work?

Phoenix and the surrounding Valley — including Mesa, Scottsdale, Tempe, Chandler, and Gilbert. Not sure if you're in range? Reach out and we'll let you know.
